# Consumer Interview Guide — Part B2 Consumer Research

For the CB course assignment's Part B2 requirement: qualitative interviews with a
minimum of 5 potential users. Goal is real behavioral insight ("people postpone
X because Y"), not generic statements ("people want to be informed").

## Recruitment

- **Minimum 5 participants.** Aim for *range*, not convenience — a heavy news
  consumer, a doom-scroller, someone who actively avoids news, someone who
  only gets news secondhand (social media, friends), etc. A sample of 5
  similar friends will produce generic, confirmatory answers; the assignment
  specifically penalizes that.
- Any adult who consumes news in some form qualifies — doesn't need to be a
  student, doesn't need to already use a news app.
- 20–25 minutes each is enough. Don't over-recruit past what you can transcribe
  and synthesize in the time you have.

## Logistics

1. **Consent, out loud, before recording starts:**
   > "I'm interviewing people for a class project on news consumption habits.
   > I'd like to record this so I can review it later — the recording and
   > anything you say will only be used for this project, and I'll refer to
   > you by a made-up name, not your real one, in anything I write up. Is
   > that okay with you?"
2. Record audio only (phone voice memo is fine). No video needed.
3. Ask questions in the order below, but let the conversation breathe —
   if an answer opens up something interesting, follow it before returning
   to the script. The order matters more for the early sections than the
   later ones: don't show the app screens (Section 7) until everything
   else is done, so the app doesn't anchor their earlier answers.
4. After each interview: transcribe it (a phone transcription app, or by
   hand — doesn't need to be verbatim-perfect, just accurate to what was
   said) and send me the transcript labeled with a pseudonym and rough
   context (e.g. "Priya, ~22, college student" — no real names, no exact
   identifying details). I'll turn the set of transcripts into the
   Insight → Evidence → Design-implication table Part B2 wants.

## Interview Script

### 1. Warm-up / context (establish real behavior, not aspiration)

- Walk me through the last time you checked the news — where were you, what
  were you doing right before, what app or source did you open?
- About how often would you say you check the news in a normal day?
- What's your go-to source or app for news? Why that one?

### 2. Intention vs. behavior gap

- Do you ever feel like you're only getting one side of a story? Tell me
  about a time that happened.
- Have you ever checked a second source to see if a story was reported the
  same way elsewhere? What made you do that (or not do that)?
- If you had to guess — do you think the news you read leans a particular
  political direction? How would you find out?

### 3. Friction / effort

- What would it take for you to check a second source on a story you just
  read? Walk me through what that would actually involve right now, with
  the apps you have.
- Has it ever occurred to you to compare sources and you just didn't,
  because it felt like a hassle? What did that feel like in the moment?
- Is there anything about the *idea* of comparing sources that seems
  annoying, boring, or not worth it to you?

### 4. Algorithm fatigue / doom-scrolling

- When you open a news or social app, is it usually because you meant to
  check something specific, or did you just end up there?
- Have you ever looked up and realized you'd been scrolling much longer
  than you meant to? What were you scrolling through?
- Do you feel like you're in control of what news you see, or like it's
  chosen for you? How do you feel about that?

### 5. Social / identity layer

- Do you ever talk about news stories with friends or family? What comes up?
- Has anyone ever disagreed with you about a news story because they'd read
  a different account of it? What happened?
- Does it matter to you whether people think you're "informed"? In what way?

### 6. Trust / bias awareness

- How do you decide whether to trust a news story or source?
- Can you think of a specific time you caught a source being biased or
  one-sided? What tipped you off?
- Do you know who owns the outlets you get news from?

### 7. Reaction to the actual app (last, unprompted first impressions)

Show 2–3 real screens (feed, a story page with multiple sources, the
compass/profile screen). Ask before explaining anything:

- What do you think this screen is for, just from looking at it?
- What would you tap first?
- Does anything feel confusing, cluttered, or unclear?

Then briefly explain what it's for and ask:

- Would you actually use something like this? Why or why not?
- What's missing, or what would make you trust it less?

### Wrap-up

- Is there anything about how you get your news that I didn't ask about but
  should have?

## After the interviews

Send me each transcript + pseudonym/context. I'll synthesize across all of
them into the assignment's required format for each major insight:

- **Observation/quote** — what was actually said or done
- **Behavioral insight** — the underlying mechanism (effort, habit, identity,
  social norm, etc.), stated the way the assignment wants: not "students want
  balanced news" but "students don't forget they want balanced news, they
  don't compare sources because the second lookup feels like unrewarded
  effort in the moment"
- **Design implication** — what this means for the app's features
